# Processing Pipeline and Bias: Claim-Triggered Audit

Ask for evidence from the actual processing pipeline; a workflow diagram, representative 2D classes, or the final map alone cannot reproduce particle selection and classification. If particle stacks, half-maps, or key parameters are unavailable, state how that limits the strength of the conclusion.

## Particle selection and classification

- Request processing records, particle counts and selection records at each round, 2D/3D classes, class proportions, classification parameters, masks, and the reconstructions used, as relevant to the claim. If different conformations or compositions came from the same experiment, check how they relate to one another. **Audit recommendation:** use these records to assess whether particle selection and classification support the specific structural interpretation. The community report strongly encourages archiving particle stacks and notes that related data and metadata help with review and reprocessing; it does not make every item above a standard deliverable for every paper. ([kleywegt2024] DOI [10.1107/S2052252524001246](https://doi.org/10.1107/S2052252524001246), §2.1 Recommendations 7–8, §3.3.6)
- Classification outputs depend on the statistical model and the number of input classes. RELION's theoretical model treats K as a known parameter, and the user sets how many models to refine simultaneously. The paper also explains that the optimization is local and that results depend on the starting model. **Audit judgment:** only when a biological conclusion depends on a discrete class, assess whether it is stable to plausible alternatives for K, initialization, or particle subsets, based on the concern and available data; seek independent experiments or clear local-density support. This is not a general requirement to rerun every paper's analysis. ([relion2012] DOI [10.1016/j.jsb.2012.09.006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsb.2012.09.006), §2.1, §5 Conclusions)
- If a paper claims that “a structural feature is present only in one class,” check the particle count, local density, and whether selection of that class was independent of discovery of the feature. If the conclusion appears only in a small subset chosen after the fact, or in a class that is unstable under reasonable processing changes, lower the strength of the conclusion. **This is an evidence judgment made by this audit, not a universal threshold or mandatory test specified by the RELION paper.** ([relion2012] DOI [10.1016/j.jsb.2012.09.006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsb.2012.09.006), §2.1, §5 Conclusions)

## Reference models, prediction priors, and symmetry

- Record the source of the starting map/model, its low-pass filtering resolution, how the reference was generated, its symmetry, the mask, the reconstruction and refinement software, and key parameters. RELION describes the algorithm as a local optimization whose result depends on the starting model; therefore, treat reference provenance as necessary context for interpreting the workflow, not as a new experimental observation. ([relion2012] DOI [10.1016/j.jsb.2012.09.006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsb.2012.09.006), §2.1, §5 Conclusions)
- Identify AlphaFold or another predicted model as a prior or modeling starting point; a good fit to the same map or plausible geometry is not independent experimental validation. The 2024 community report notes that PDB training data may give prediction models information about known structural packing and considers the related validation methods to be in need of further development. **Audit recommendation:** if the predicted model supports a key claim, seek complementary evidence such as local density and independent data. ([kleywegt2024] DOI [10.1107/S2052252524001246](https://doi.org/10.1107/S2052252524001246), §5; [relion2012] DOI [10.1016/j.jsb.2012.09.006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsb.2012.09.006), §1, §2.1)
- If a model was repeatedly flexibly fitted to the map, check whether it remains consistent with data that were not used in fitting. Challenge rule #4 **strongly encourages** participants to fit an unsharpened, unmasked map or one half-map; however, the study explicitly says it did not evaluate overfitting with the half-map method. Thus, this is modeling guidance, not a validated cross-validation effect. **Audit inference:** if a conclusion depends on the fitted result, inspect the other half-map that was not used for fitting or other independent evidence; the extent of the check depends on the claim and data availability. ([em-challenge2021] DOI [10.1038/s41592-020-01051-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-020-01051-w), Methods “Challenge process and organization,” challenge rules #4; Discussion; [kleywegt2024] DOI [10.1107/S2052252524001246](https://doi.org/10.1107/S2052252524001246), §3.4 Recommendation 15)
- Check the point group used for reconstruction, the standard symmetry convention, and the symmetry of the model and map. The 2024 community report recommends validating the map's point-group information and convention. **Audit judgment:** if the conclusion concerns asymmetric occupancy, an asymmetric functional state, or a missing subunit, assess a lower-symmetry reconstruction or independent data as appropriate to the claim; symmetry-sensitivity analysis is not a fixed threshold for every study. ([kleywegt2024] DOI [10.1107/S2052252524001246](https://doi.org/10.1107/S2052252524001246), §3.3.5)

## Independent validation in cryo-EM and X-ray crystallography is not interchangeable

- Cryo-EM half-map FSC compares the consistency of reconstructions from independent halves of the data; map–model FSC compares the experimental map with a map calculated from the model, so they validate different things. Global map–model FSC provides information about overall fit. **Audit judgment:** it cannot replace local checks of a specific residue or ligand. The challenge study found that single-residue and sliding-window metrics behaved differently, and that local-fit metrics at near-atomic resolution still need to be interpreted for the specific map. ([kleywegt2024] DOI [10.1107/S2052252524001246](https://doi.org/10.1107/S2052252524001246), §3.3.1, §3.4, Recommendation 15; [em-challenge2021] DOI [10.1038/s41592-020-01051-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-020-01051-w), Figure 3d, Results “Evaluating metrics: local scoring”)
- X-ray R-free measures agreement between observed and calculated structure-factor amplitudes on test reflections held out during model refinement. Its target, data, and numerical scale differ from those of half-map FSC. Do not substitute R-free for a map–model test or compare the numerical values of FSC = 0.143, map–model FSC = 0.5, R-free, or CC1/2/CC*. ([brunger1992] DOI [10.1038/355472a0](https://doi.org/10.1038/355472a0), pp. 472–475, text and figure comparing cross-validation; [karplus2012] DOI [10.1126/science.1218231](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1218231), text pp. 1030–1033, Figures 1–4; [kleywegt2024] DOI [10.1107/S2052252524001246](https://doi.org/10.1107/S2052252524001246), §3.4)
- For both methods, report map/data independence and the validation target. Withheld reflections for X-ray R-free and half-maps for cryo-EM are cross-validation designs with related concepts, but they are not the same statistic. If raw validation data are unavailable, limit the assessment to the published map/model and reported information. ([brunger1992] DOI [10.1038/355472a0](https://doi.org/10.1038/355472a0), pp. 472–475; [scheres-chen2012] DOI [10.1038/nmeth.2115](https://doi.org/10.1038/nmeth.2115), Figure 1; [kleywegt2024] DOI [10.1107/S2052252524001246](https://doi.org/10.1107/S2052252524001246), §2.1 Recommendations 2–3, §3.3.1)
