# X-ray Crystallography: Auditing Data and Precision Claims

Use this guide to check claims about X-ray structure resolution, data quality, refinement, and coordinate precision. Prioritize the paper, PDB entry, and its validation report. If reflection data are inaccessible, state which data-level checks could not be performed; do not treat missing information as an error.

## Data completeness, anisotropy, and resolution limits

- Check completeness, redundancy, and signal-to-noise indicators overall and in the highest-resolution shell; if the conclusion depends on anomalous signal, also record anomalous completeness. Severe omissions in a shell or direction can limit the corresponding claims, but completeness alone cannot establish that a model is wrong. ([evans2013](https://doi.org/10.1107/S0907444913000061), §3.1, Figure 1, and §3.2.3)
- Inspect how CC1/2 changes across resolution shells. If the data are strongly anisotropic, compare support from the data along major directions or directional sectors where possible; do not assume that a single isotropic cutoff implies equal information in every direction. Rmerge/Rmeas are not suitable as the sole criterion for a high-resolution cutoff. ([evans2013](https://doi.org/10.1107/S0907444913000061), §§3.2.2–3.2.3, Figure 2; [karplus2012](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1218231), Figure 2)
- For a disputed cutoff, compare adjacent cutoffs using paired refinement. To make the comparison fair, use the same starting model, the same free flags, and the same refinement protocol, then calculate Rwork and Rfree for both models over the common resolution range at the lower-resolution cutoff. Assess whether the added data are useful in conjunction with CC1/2, difference maps, and model changes; do not apply a single universal CC1/2 threshold. Karplus and Diederichs illustrate paired refinement by comparing Rwork/Rfree over the common resolution range; Evans and Murshudov also recommend it as a supplementary check when deciding resolution limits. ([karplus2012](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1218231), Figure 1, p. 1031; [evans2013](https://doi.org/10.1107/S0907444913000061), §5, p. 1211)

## Rwork, Rfree, and independence

- Check Rwork, Rfree, the refinement resolution, and the size of the free set together. The cross-validation interpretation of Rfree depends on test reflections that were not used to fit the model. For re-refinement and model comparisons, preserve the same free flags; do not reshuffle them, and do not treat a test set repeatedly used to select models as an independent test. Also check that the flags are compatible with lattice symmetry and twinning laws; otherwise, the work and test sets may become statistically dependent and Rfree may be artificially lowered. ([brunger1992](https://doi.org/10.1038/355472a0), pp. 472–475; [read2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.str.2011.08.006), p. 1404; [karplus2012](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1218231), p. 1031)
- The Rwork/Rfree gap, their absolute values, or a decrease in a single refinement round cannot by themselves establish that a local structure is correct. Treat them as global metrics and audit them together with data quality, the refinement protocol, and the local evidence relevant to the claim. If the history of the free set cannot be confirmed, record that its independence “cannot be verified.” ([evans2013](https://doi.org/10.1107/S0907444913000061), §5, p. 1211; [read2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.str.2011.08.006), p. 1404)

## Resolution is not coordinate precision

- Distinguish resolution (the highest spatial frequency in the reflection data) from coordinate precision or coordinate uncertainty. Nominal resolution describes the data cutoff; it is not the positional error for each atom and cannot by itself support a claim that “coordinates are accurate to X Å.” ([evans2013](https://doi.org/10.1107/S0907444913000061), §5; [cruickshank1999](https://doi.org/10.1107/S0907444998012645), §6.4, Eq. 30, and §10.2)
- If discussing DPI or σ(r), interpret DPI as a rough estimate of the diffraction-data contribution to coordinate uncertainty, as discussed in §10.2; do not treat it as a measured error for every atom. Use the erratum when citing or recalculating: in Eq. 30, the exponent on C is corrected from −1/2 to −1/3; in Eq. 49, the sum-of-squared-residuals term must include the weight w. The erratum also corrects notation in §4.2 on p. 589 and the caption of Figure 7. ([cruickshank1999](https://doi.org/10.1107/S0907444998012645), §6.4, Eq. 30, §7.1, §10.2, Appendix A1 Eq. 49; [cruickshank1999-erratum](https://doi.org/10.1107/S0907444999004308), p. 1108)

## Audit record

Record the PDB accession, the version or revision date of the coordinate and reflection files used, the data source, the reported resolution limits, and the location of the validation report. For a specific atom, record `_atom_site.auth_asym_id`/`_atom_site.label_asym_id`, `auth_seq_id`/`label_seq_id`, `pdbx_PDB_ins_code`, `auth_atom_id`/`label_atom_id`, and `label_alt_id`. If the paper's numbering differs from the deposited file, document the mapping.
