# Single-Particle Cryo-EM (SPA): Claim-Triggered Audit

This page covers single-particle analysis (SPA). Other modalities, such as cryo-electron tomography, require separate assessment. Audit specific structural claims. If half-maps, FSC curves, or method details are unavailable, state which check cannot be performed and assign one of the four verdicts according to the accessible evidence. Missing half-maps can prevent independent recalculation while a processing report still supports a narrowly worded reported value. Polished renderings do not replace experimental evidence.

The questions below are triggered by particular claims; they are not a requirement that every paper provide every analysis. Citations identify facts directly supported by the source. Audit suggestions that extend beyond those facts are explicitly labeled as audit judgments and should be pursued according to the importance of the claim and the available data.

## “The structure is resolved at 2.8 Å”

- Review the half-maps, the complete half-map FSC curve, the threshold used, and the processing records. The source directly supports refining models separately against the two data half-sets to avoid half-map pseudo-correlation; **this audit operationalizes that practice as follows:** trace reconstruction and refinement from initialization through FSC calculation, checking where high-resolution information could have crossed between halves. Record the starting reference and its low-pass filtering, including whether both halves shared a low-pass-filtered initialization. A shared initializer is not an automatic failure: assess its effective resolution and potential to propagate correlated signal or noise. This does not require duplicating all preprocessing, such as particle picking. A gold-standard workflow can reduce overfitting risk, but the actual processing steps still need to be assessed. ([scheres-chen2012] DOI [10.1038/nmeth.2115](https://doi.org/10.1038/nmeth.2115), Figure 1; [kleywegt2024] DOI [10.1107/S2052252524001246](https://doi.org/10.1107/S2052252524001246), §1.3, §3.3.1)
- Distinguish what each metric compares: half-map FSC compares independent half-maps and is commonly reported at 0.143; map–model FSC compares the experimental map with a density map calculated from model coordinates and is commonly reported at 0.5. They address different questions, so their values are not interchangeable. Map–model FSC can be affected by the mask and simulation parameters. **Audit judgment:** global map–model FSC alone cannot validate a particular residue, ligand, or atomic model. If a claim depends on local detail, also inspect the relevant local density and other applicable evidence. ([scheres-chen2012] DOI [10.1038/nmeth.2115](https://doi.org/10.1038/nmeth.2115), Figure 1; [kleywegt2024] DOI [10.1107/S2052252524001246](https://doi.org/10.1107/S2052252524001246), §3.4, Recommendation 15; [em-challenge2021] DOI [10.1038/s41592-020-01051-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-020-01051-w), Figure 3d, Results “Evaluating metrics: local scoring”)
- Rosenthal–Henderson derived 0.143 under assumptions that include uncorrelated signal and noise: in their model, a half-map FSC of 0.143 corresponds to a correlation of about 0.5 between the full-data map and an ideal noiseless reference map. This does not mean that the half-map FSC itself is 0.5, or that map–model FSC is 0.5; it is a conversion under a specific statistical model. ([rh2003] DOI [10.1016/j.jmb.2003.07.013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmb.2003.07.013), Appendix, Eqs. A2–A6, Table A1)
- The statistical interpretation of FSC thresholds remains contested. van Heel and Schatz argue that the meaning of a fixed threshold changes with the number of Fourier samples per shell, symmetry, and the fraction of the reconstruction volume occupied by the object. They distinguish an r-factor curve for statistical significance above random noise from a half-bit curve indicating enough information for interpretation, and argue that 0.143 is only an approximation under asymptotic conditions. The 2024 community report recommends presenting the 0.143, 0.5, and half-bit results together. **Audit recommendation:** prioritize the full curve and information on methods, masks, and half-map independence; compare alternative thresholds if the choice could change the conclusion. Do not present any one number as a hard limit that applies to all data. ([vanheel2005] DOI [10.1016/j.jsb.2005.05.009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsb.2005.05.009), §2.2–2.4, §4.2–4.7, §5–6, Eqs. 14–17, Figure 4C; [kleywegt2024] DOI [10.1107/S2052252524001246](https://doi.org/10.1107/S2052252524001246), §3.1)

## Half-map independence, masks, and overfitting

- Inspect the mask files and how they were used; when available, also inspect unmasked, unfiltered half-maps. A mask can change FSC by removing background; a mask that is too tight or has edges that are too sharp can introduce high-frequency spurious correlation. For resolution estimates using a custom mask, the 2024 community report recommends also showing a noise-substitution-corrected curve. Chen et al.'s Equation (4) and Figure 4 show how noise-substitution correction can separate true correlation from mask-induced spurious correlation. The authors also note that sharper edges, especially in a binary mask, cause larger artifacts and can make the correction inaccurate. Therefore, interpret the corrected curve together with the mask and method; do not treat it as a universally valid remedy. ([kleywegt2024] DOI [10.1107/S2052252524001246](https://doi.org/10.1107/S2052252524001246), §2.1 Recommendations 2–3, §3.4 Recommendation 15; [chen2013] DOI [10.1016/j.ultramic.2013.06.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ultramic.2013.06.004), §1.2, §2, §3, Eq. 4, Figure 4)
- If the workflow may overfit high-resolution noise, or if the FSC conclusion is sensitive to a custom mask, consider asking for a high-resolution noise-substitution control, including high-frequency phase randomization. Compare the original curve, the noise-control curve, and, where applicable, the corrected curve. Chen et al. show that this test can be used with different processing programs, but that the noise-substitution resolution cutoff must be chosen appropriately. This is a check for a specific concern, not a standard workflow required for every study. Without evidence of independent half-map processing or correction information for a custom mask, do not treat a masked curve crossing 0.143 as sufficient evidence by itself. ([chen2013] DOI [10.1016/j.ultramic.2013.06.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ultramic.2013.06.004), §1.2, §2–3, §4–5, Figures 2–4; [scheres-chen2012] DOI [10.1038/nmeth.2115](https://doi.org/10.1038/nmeth.2115), Figure 1)

## Local resolution, directionality, and post-processing

- A global resolution value does not establish that a local ligand, flexible loop, or interface has the same clarity. If a structural claim depends on a local feature, inspect the density for that region, local consistency between the two half-maps, and a local-resolution map. ResMap defines local resolution in terms of whether a local sinusoidal feature is detectable at a given P value, with a correction for multiple testing; this is not equivalent to per-atom model accuracy. The 2024 community report notes that current local-resolution algorithms and parameters do not ensure quantitative comparability across situations and recommends displaying the values qualitatively, from “better” to “worse.” ([kucukelbir2014] DOI [10.1038/nmeth.2727](https://doi.org/10.1038/nmeth.2727), main text, Figure 1, Online Methods; [kleywegt2024] DOI [10.1107/S2052252524001246](https://doi.org/10.1107/S2052252524001246), §3.3.2)
- When particle angular distributions are strongly uneven, or a claim depends on details in a particular direction, check the Euler-angle distribution and inspect 3D FSC, directional FSC, or another directional metric. Record the threshold used and the weakest direction. Tan et al. show the relationship between preferred orientation, directional resolution, and reconstruction artifacts. The angular distribution describes sampling; directional FSC describes directional consistency in the reconstructed map. They are not substitutes for each other. The 2024 community report notes that directional algorithms still need systematic comparison and community consensus, so no single algorithm should be treated as a fixed threshold for every project. ([tan2017] DOI [10.1038/nmeth.4347](https://doi.org/10.1038/nmeth.4347), Figures 1 and 2, Online Methods “Three-dimensional Fourier shell correlation algorithm”; [kleywegt2024] DOI [10.1107/S2052252524001246](https://doi.org/10.1107/S2052252524001246), §3.3.3)
- If post-processing may affect interpretation of detail, compare unfiltered, unsharpened half-maps or the raw map with the map shown in the paper, and record RAPS, the sharpening method, and B-factor parameters if provided. The 2024 community report recommends comparing RAPS for the primary and raw maps to help assess filtering and post-processing. **This audit treats those records as context for interpreting image appearance:** a sharper display is not itself evidence of additional information. Also record the contour level when viewing a density map, because atom-inclusion scores depend on that threshold. ([kleywegt2024] DOI [10.1107/S2052252524001246](https://doi.org/10.1107/S2052252524001246), §2.1 Recommendation 2, §3.3.4, §3.4)
- Check the point group and symmetry convention used for reconstruction, and the symmetry of the model and map. The 2024 community report explicitly recommends validating the map's point group and convention. **Audit judgment:** if the claim concerns asymmetric occupancy or an asymmetric conformation, ask for a lower-symmetry reconstruction or other independent evidence as appropriate to the claim; a map reconstructed with imposed symmetry cannot itself validate that asymmetry. ([kleywegt2024] DOI [10.1107/S2052252524001246](https://doi.org/10.1107/S2052252524001246), §3.3.5)
