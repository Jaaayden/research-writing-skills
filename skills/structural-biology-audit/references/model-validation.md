# Atomic Models: Geometry, Local Fit, and Sequence Audit

Model auditing separately examines the experimental data, the coordinate model, and the model’s fit to the data. Global scores can hide rare or local outliers and cannot replace local evidence for the residue or atom being discussed; an individual outlier is a lead for investigation, and a good score on one metric does not prove correctness. ([afonine2018](https://doi.org/10.1107/S2059798318009324), §2.1; [read2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.str.2011.08.006), pp. 1396–1397, 1407–1410, Fig. 6C, Table 4)

## Geometry and conformation

- Inspect bond lengths, bond angles, planarity, chirality, Ramachandran and rotamer conformations, clashes, and local backbone conformation in the target region; interpret outliers in light of resolution, model type, refinement restraints, and the reference set used in the report. Bond-length/bond-angle residuals are affected by refinement restraints; common geometry close to ideal values is not independent validation, while Ramachandran and rotamer metrics are generally less directly restrained. Some outliers may represent real conformations, so return to the experimental data. ([read2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.str.2011.08.006), pp. 1397–1401, Figs. 1–3; [afonine2018](https://doi.org/10.1107/S2059798318009324), §3.1, Fig. 2; [em-challenge2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-020-01051-w), “Overall and local quality of models,” Extended Data Figs. 1–3)
- Check local density/model fit alongside conformational evidence. If geometry conflicts with density, report the conflict and its specific location rather than deciding from one global geometry score; local fit scores such as RSR and RSCC are also affected by the calculation region, resolution, and selected map. ([read2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.str.2011.08.006), pp. 1404–1405; [afonine2018](https://doi.org/10.1107/S2059798318009324), §2.1, §3.1, §3.3)
- Check peptide-bond orientation and ω. About 5% of proline peptide bonds are cis, compared with about 0.03% of non-proline peptide bonds; strongly twisted non-planar peptide bonds are rarer still and generally warrant review first. Rare does not mean wrong: use local density, hydrogen bonds, or homologous structures to assess whether the conformation is real. ([molprobity2018](https://doi.org/10.1002/pro.3330), “Cis or twisted non-trans peptides,” pp. 305–306, Figs. 9, 11B; [em-challenge2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-020-01051-w), Results: “Overall and local quality of models,” Extended Data Fig. 1)

## Local map/model fit

- Inspect density and local fit metrics at each residue relevant to the claim; record the map, mask, sharpening/contour conditions, and local metric used. Whole-map correlation coefficients or overall resolution cannot substitute for a single-residue fit. For cryo-EM local scores, prioritize per-residue results; do not let a sliding-window average hide a short misaligned segment. ([read2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.str.2011.08.006), pp. 1404–1405; [afonine2018](https://doi.org/10.1107/S2059798318009324), §§2.1.1, 3.3, 3.5, Figs. 6–8; [em-challenge2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-020-01051-w), Recommendation 2, “Evaluating metrics: local scoring”)
- Combine geometry and fit as complementary evidence. If a metric is itself an optimization target in refinement, do not count it alone as independent validation; use other local metrics or available independent data/half-maps as cross-checks. Occupancy, B factor, mask, and map sharpening affect some fit scores. Afonine et al. also show that CCmask may remain unchanged or decrease slightly as model geometry improves, illustrating why fit values and geometry need joint interpretation. ([read2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.str.2011.08.006), p. 1404; [afonine2018](https://doi.org/10.1107/S2059798318009324), §§3.3–3.5, 3.9, Figs. 6–8, Table 4; [em-challenge2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-020-01051-w), Results: “Evaluating metrics: Fit-to-Map”)

## Sequence, register, and coordinate identity

- Check the construct/target sequence, deposited polymer sequence, chain breaks, residue numbering, and local side-chain/backbone support in the map. Record the accession, `_pdbx_audit_revision_history.revision_date` or file snapshot used, map/reflection-data accession, `_atom_site.auth_asym_id`/`_atom_site.label_asym_id`, `auth_seq_id`/`label_seq_id`, `pdbx_PDB_ins_code`, `auth_atom_id`/`label_atom_id`, and `label_alt_id`; where needed, explain the mapping between paper numbering and file numbering. ([read2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.str.2011.08.006), Fig. 6C, Table 4; [checkmysequence2022](https://doi.org/10.1107/S2059798322005009), Fig. 1)
- Distinguish the evidence sources of two register-screening approaches. checkMySequence is map-dependent and requires a cryo-EM map, coordinate model, and chain sequences from the model; it uses the map and backbone coordinates to infer residue types, then reports a p-value for the sequence assignment. This p-value estimates the probability of obtaining the result by chance; it is not the posterior probability that the model has an incorrect register. The authors’ 99.5% one-sided cutoff is explicitly an empirical threshold. They also note that lower local resolution requires longer test segments (20 by default, up to 60 residues), so short local shifts, typically fewer than about 10 residues, may be missed. ([checkmysequence2022](https://doi.org/10.1107/S2059798322005009), §2, pp. 807–808; §§4.1–4.2, pp. 809–810; §5, p. 815)
- conkit-validate is instead a map-independent screen: it compares residue contacts in the model with a distance/contact map predicted by AlphaFold2. The paper analyzed PDB cryo-EM/MX structures deposited by 2022-04-05 with nominal resolutions of 3–5 Å; the authors call the results putative register errors. AlphaFold2 prediction quality, conformational differences, and fold-switching can all produce false positives. The alternative fold of human calcineurin (PDB 5c1v), chain B, was supported by density but flagged as a potential error; automatic replacement worsened density fit, a clear false-positive example. ([register-errors2024](https://doi.org/10.1107/S2052252524009114), §§2.1–2.3, pp. 939–941; §3.5.1, p. 945; §3.6.3, pp. 946–948)
- When comparing models before and after register correction, state the experimental map/data and metrics used for coordinate replacement, local real-space fitting, and re-refinement. The paper re-refined the original and alternative models separately, then compared local CC/RSCC against the same experimental data; improved fit can be supporting evidence, but because fit and evaluation depend on the same experimental data, it cannot be called independent validation. ([register-errors2024](https://doi.org/10.1107/S2052252524009114), §2.5, p. 941; §3.4, p. 945, Fig. 5)

## Reporting limits

For each item, state the observed evidence, its source, and what is missing; if images/maps are absent or the validation report is unavailable, write “unable to verify.” Do not extrapolate static fit or contacts in a structural model into proof of dynamics, causality, or mechanism.
