# Ligands, Local Density, and Metal Sites

Use this guide when auditing claims such as “the ligand is resolved” / “clearly resolved,” “the pose is supported by density,” “a specific interaction forms,” or an Mg²⁺ assignment. Keep findings reported directly by the paper separate from audit judgments in this guide; do not treat one metric or one visualization line as a conclusion.

## Confirm the map type before examining local evidence

| Method | Evidence to inspect | Limits on interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| X-ray | Electron density calculated from reflection data, difference maps, and ligand RSCC/RSR; a conventional OMIT or polder map can be calculated. | RSCC/RSR are local fit metrics produced by a specific calculation workflow, not chemical evidence independent of the data, map, and software definitions. Polder is an OMIT method for the X-ray bulk-solvent mask.([read2011] DOI 10.1016/j.str.2011.08.006, “Ligands”; [polder2017] DOI 10.1107/S2059798316018210, §§1–2) |
| Cryo-EM | The experimental cryo-EM map, local fit of the ligand and its pocket, local resolution, and atomic resolvability scores; inspect the displayed contour level and processing information. | Whole-map FSC resolution cannot substitute for local resolution or atomic resolvability at the ligand. Q-score is calculated from the map profile around atoms in the fitted model; it does not validate ligand chemistry or stereochemistry.([pintilie2020] DOI 10.1038/s41592-020-0731-1, Introduction, “Q-score,” Discussion; [ligand-challenge2024] DOI 10.1038/s41592-024-02321-7, Discussion, Recommendations) |

**Finding reported by the paper:** The three cryo-EM maps shown in the Ligand Challenge use different contour levels. Local map evidence for a ligand is also affected by local resolution, occupancy, multiple conformations, flexibility/disorder, chemical modifications, and radiation damage.([ligand-challenge2024] DOI 10.1038/s41592-024-02321-7, Fig. 2 caption, Discussion)

**Audit judgment:** Record the map source, processing/sharpening version, local resolution at the ligand, and displayed contour (σ or map value). Inspect the ligand and nearby pocket under the same settings; check whether the density is continuous and distinguishes key atoms, rings, and substituents. Do not treat a shape that appears only after lowering the contour as proof that the ligand is clearly resolved. If different contour levels are shown side by side, label each value and state why those views are shown. Do not set a universal pass threshold across datasets. Where possible, compare unsharpened and processed maps. Pintilie et al. report in the main text that sharpening can increase visible detail and Q-score, but that Q-score decreases after excessive sharpening; the main text points to Supplementary Fig. 5 for this result, and that supplement was not independently checked here.([pintilie2020] DOI 10.1038/s41592-020-0731-1, “Q-scores of atoms in proteins,” PDF p. 3 (description in the main text; Supplementary Fig. 5 not independently checked); [ligand-challenge2024] DOI 10.1038/s41592-024-02321-7, Fig. 2 caption)

## Check density, model chemistry, and alternative explanations

- **Inspect local fit fragment by fragment.** Compare local support for the ligand, surrounding protein/nucleic acid, ions, and waters; do not report only a whole-model or whole-molecule average. The Ligand Challenge found that local ligand/ligand-environment fit-to-map metrics and coordinates-only metrics can be independent of one another. The current Phenix `validate_ligands` documentation (checked 2026-09-30) describes X-ray validation: it reports RSCC for the whole ligand and for rigid fragments separately, and provides `sites` RSCC for the environment within 3 Å of the ligand. Thus, an acceptable overall score can still mask weak local density.([ligand-challenge2024] DOI 10.1038/s41592-024-02321-7, Discussion, Fig. 3, Extended Data Fig. 3B, Supplementary Data S3; [official Phenix documentation](https://www.phenix-online.org/documentation/reference/validate_ligands.html), “How it works,” “Metrics,” “Possible Problems,” accessed 2026-09-30)
- **Check chemistry and stereochemistry.** Verify ligand identity/CCD, atom completeness, connectivity and covalent links; inspect bond lengths, bond angles, planarity, chirality, ring conformation, strain, clashes, and surrounding contacts. Plausible geometry does not establish density support, and good fit does not establish correct geometry.([ligand-challenge2024] DOI 10.1038/s41592-024-02321-7, Discussion, Recommendations)
- **Compare alternative explanations.** Check whether density could instead come from water, buffer/crystallization additives, another pose, or partially occupied multiple conformations. Weak density can also result from disorder/flexibility rather than an incorrect ligand identity.([polder2017] DOI 10.1107/S2059798316018210, §3.1.2, §5, Fig. 5; [ligand-challenge2024] DOI 10.1038/s41592-024-02321-7, Results, Fig. 5, Extended Data Fig. 4A–B, Discussion)

## Fit metrics from X-ray and cryo-EM are not directly interchangeable

- **State how RSCC/RSR was calculated.** The wwPDB X-ray VTF by Read et al. recommends reporting ligand geometry outliers and local density fit; it notes that an overall score for a large ligand can mask local problems and recommends comparing ligand RSR with the protein portion. The paper does not define cryo-EM RSCC or prescribe a universal RSCC threshold for all ligands.([read2011] DOI 10.1016/j.str.2011.08.006, “Ligands”)
- **Interpret a specific RSCC according to the software documentation.** Current Phenix documentation explains that X-ray RSCC compares the `2mFo−DFc` map calculated after omitting the ligand with the `DFmodel` map calculated with the ligand present, over grid points within 1.5 Å of ligand non-hydrogen atoms. It also reports scores for the whole ligand, rigid fragments, and `sites` scores for protein/DNA/RNA atoms within 3 Å. The page’s main use, method, metrics, and problem descriptions are for X-ray; it states that the tool is intended for models refined against X-ray data. The presence of a cryo-EM map `resolution` parameter in the keyword list is not enough to define a cryo-EM RSCC algorithm. Record the actual map, software, and calculation definition; do not extrapolate this X-ray workflow to cryo-EM or compare values directly across data types or software.([official Phenix documentation](https://www.phenix-online.org/documentation/reference/validate_ligands.html), “Usage,” “How it works,” “Metrics,” “Possible Problems,” “List of all available keywords,” accessed 2026-09-30)
- **OMIT/polder maps are crystallographic evidence.** Calculating an X-ray OMIT map after removing the atoms under examination can reduce the model’s influence on its own density; the bulk-solvent mask may enter the vacated OMIT region and obscure weak signal. Polder excludes bulk solvent from the OMIT region and may enhance a weak ligand signal.([polder2017] DOI 10.1107/S2059798316018210, Abstract, §§1–3.1, Figs. 1–6)
- **OMIT/polder maps are not independent proof.** In a compact pocket, polder can show solvent or the pocket shape as density resembling the atoms under examination; the paper shows that a fictitious ligand can also produce positive peaks. Inspect conventional OMIT maps, polder maps, difference maps, alternative explanations, and local chemistry together; do not infer ligand presence from continuous polder peaks alone.([polder2017] DOI 10.1107/S2059798316018210, §5, Table 2, Fig. 2)
- **For cryo-EM, inspect map-local evidence.** Q-score measures the similarity between the map profile around an atom and a reference Gaussian; it depends on the model’s fitted position and does not test stereochemistry. The paper shows that Q-score can still be high when the surface at a selected contour does not enclose an atom, so also inspect the displayed contour, local map, resolution, and model chemistry.([pintilie2020] DOI 10.1038/s41592-020-0731-1, “Q-score,” Fig. 3, Discussion)
- **B factors/ADPs and occupancy are not independent proof from density.** Check the refinement strategy and compare ligand ADPs/B factors with the nearby environment; current Phenix documentation, in its X-ray validation context, reports ADP statistics for the ligand and the surrounding 3 Å environment, as well as ligand occupancy. Even when cryo-EM model ADPs are listed, they are often constrained by restraints and should not be treated as a direct measure of local map strength. The Ligand Challenge recommends judging both the absolute strength of local density and its strength relative to the surrounding environment, and cites half-strength density for F86 as supporting approximately 50% occupancy; this is a case-specific example, not a general calibration formula. Also consider flexibility, local resolution, multiple poses, and refinement parameters when interpreting the result.([pintilie2020] DOI 10.1038/s41592-020-0731-1, Introduction, Discussion; [ligand-challenge2024] DOI 10.1038/s41592-024-02321-7, Discussion, Extended Data Fig. 4B; [official Phenix documentation](https://www.phenix-online.org/documentation/reference/validate_ligands.html), “Metrics,” accessed 2026-09-30)

## Mg²⁺: inspect the coordination shell; do not assign the ion from distance alone

- **Assess identity and coordination together.** Check the map, coordinating atom types/charges, coordination number, geometry, nearby waters, occupancy/B factor, and sample buffer composition; compare alternatives such as Na⁺, water, and other ions. CheckMyMetal reports that Mg²⁺ commonly has near-octahedral sixfold coordination and Mg—O distances around 2.08 Å, but also shows an example where a similar distance is more consistent with Na⁺. Typical geometry is not an identity threshold.([cmm2017] DOI 10.1107/S2059798317001061, §2, §§3.1.1–3.1.2, Fig. 2, Table 1)
- **Check for restraint circularity.** CMM 2017 reports that the Mg—O default restraint in the refinement library at that time was 2.18 Å, while the peak in the CSD distribution was around 2.08 Å. If restraints have pulled the coordinates toward the expected value, that distance is no longer independent evidence for ion identity. Check the actual software/dictionary version and inspect density, coordination chemistry, and alternative ions independently. Do not assume the paper’s reported default represents the current library.([cmm2017] DOI 10.1107/S2059798317001061, §3.1.2, Table 2)
- **Distinguish method scope.** CMM coordination-shell/valence/completeness/geometry metrics differ from its occupancy and B-factor metrics; the paper explicitly states that the latter two are X-ray-specific. If the synchrotron wavelength can be tuned near the metal absorption edge, anomalous scattering can help identify the element; this is a conditional X-ray method, not a general EM check.([cmm2017] DOI 10.1107/S2059798317001061, §§1–2)
- **Do not extrapolate the nucleic-acid survey.** Leonarski et al. surveyed nucleic-acid PDB structures deposited through May 2016 at resolutions of ≤3 Å, focusing on direct inner-sphere contacts between Mg²⁺ and nucleobase imine N1/N3/N7 atoms. The survey did not examine water-mediated outer-sphere Mg—N7 contacts and does not cover all nucleic-acid Mg²⁺ sites or coordination modes. Many reported direct contacts were more likely to be Na⁺, K⁺, NH₄⁺, water, or noise peaks; the conclusion cannot be generalized to protein Mg sites.([leonarski2017] DOI 10.1093/nar/gkw1175, Abstract, Introduction, “PDB survey,” Discussion)
